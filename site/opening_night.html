Research project · forecasting box office

Opening Night

What predicts a movie's opening weekend now, and do stars still matter? A pre-registered model of 1,959 US wide releases, tested on films it had never seen and graded live this season.

Live season: Oct–Dec 2026 Confirmatory run: Oct 19, 2026 Data 2010–2025 · opened in 800+ theaters

The short version

Box office lore says stars sell tickets and franchises are safe bets. I tested whether that still holds after 2020, and whether audience attention before release explains the hits and flops that the usual factors miss.

Every hypothesis was written down before any data was collected. 2025 was sealed away during development and opened exactly once, after the model was frozen in a git commit.

Main finding: star track records barely predicted openings before 2020 and still don't. One signal held up after correcting for multiple tests: films whose Wikipedia attention speeds up in the last two weeks before release beat the traditional forecast.

How it was done

Data. Opening weekends and theater counts for every film that opened in 800+ theaters, 2010–2025, from Box Office Mojo (copied by hand). Genre, rating, cast, director and franchise from TMDB. Daily Wikipedia page views before release for the film and, for adaptations, its source material.

Only what was knowable. Every input is something a forecaster could have known before release. Track records count only films at least a week older. Production budget was dropped, because databases fill it in after release.

Tested like real forecasting. Each quarter from 2014 to 2025, the models were retrained on earlier films only, then forecast the films opening that quarter. Two checkpoints: four weeks out (T-28) and one week out, once the theater count is announced (T-7).

Limits. Attention may partly reflect marketing spend, which isn't public. Theater counts are set by studios that already expect a certain result. These are associations, not causes, and the post-2020 sample is a few hundred films.

Data: Box Office Mojo (opening figures), TMDB, Wikipedia/Wikidata. This product uses the TMDB API but is not endorsed or certified by TMDB. Hypotheses, amendments and code live in a version-controlled repository; raw box office tables are not published.
